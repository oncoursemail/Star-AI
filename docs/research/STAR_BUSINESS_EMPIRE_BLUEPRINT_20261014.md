# STAR Business Empire — Research and Implementation Blueprint

**Research date:** 2026-10-14  
**Status:** Strategic research and proposed implementation; **not an operating storefront, customer base, or revenue report**.  
**Code grounding:** Public `oncoursemail/Star-AI` branch `star/next` and `androoAGI/starnet` branch `feat/harness-backend`, read through connected GitHub repository access.

## Executive assessment

STAR should become an **evidence-driven business production system** with three revenue legs: (1) scoped high-quality client services for early cashflow and real feedback; (2) re-usable original digital products built from proven needs; and (3) subscriptions and maintenance offerings where repeat demand is demonstrated. Print-on-demand, affiliate content and AI music are secondary experiments, **not the first priority**.

This is **not a promise to generate revenue**. The founder has to open accounts, approve expenditures, legally own assets, make sales, fulfill commitments and maintain quality. Agents can propose, draft, assemble, inspect and report. Real outcomes are based on authenticated payment/order records rather than self-reported assistant messages.

## What the video model gets right — and what STAR can improve

The androoAGI StarNet model treats each pixel-art room as a real permission/capability scope, each hallway as authorized workflow handoff and each agent as a distinct model/tool run. Its public README describes real task work, sidecar execution, local spending ledgers, approvals, memory, scheduling, and multi-agent parallelism. StarNet's **code is MIT licensed**, but its brand and artwork are explicitly excluded. Copying the creator's footage/station art or business case as verified earnings would be inappropriate.

Improvements to prioritize:

1. **Profit-first orchestration**, not only task orchestration: source-connected margins per product, marketing costs, refunds, labor, taxes, and uncertainty.
2. **A validation gate before production**: concrete buyer problem, demand evidence, willingness-to-pay interviews, competition, rights, costs and planned distribution.
3. **A verification gate before delivery**: playable Roblox test place, reproducible Luau tests, frame/screenshot comparisons, device performance and legal/asset checks.
4. **Strictly honest accounting**: paid orders, pending orders, funds available, gross revenue, contribution profit and withdrawn payouts are separate fields.
5. **Human-controlled commercial authority**: no autonomous unapproved ad spend, purchases, refund promises, public publishing, customer messaging at scale or financial transfers.
6. **Differentiated original products**: tools that save working developers hours, not bulk generic AI designs.
7. **Customer learning**: turns support tickets, nonfraudulent feedback, usage and failures into versioned product improvement proposals.

## Public market research, October 2026

- Roblox Creator Store: verified USD monetization for **Models ($2.99–$49.99)** and **Plugins ($4.99–$249.99)**. Roblox says sellers earn 100% of net proceeds after applicable taxes/processing; an approved Stripe seller account and 30-day escrow are required. The free VFX Studio plugin and the paid Sine VFX ($7.99) show actual competing tool categories; ratings/reviews do not equal unit sales.
- Unity Asset Store: publisher retains 70% of Asset Store revenue; publisher vetting and store support requirements apply. Live tool category listings include editor helpers, dialogue, animation and saving systems at various prices.
- Epic Fab: creator receives 88% revenue share on eligible marketplace content.
- Framer: approved creators can publish paid templates/plugins/components, with 100% of Marketplace template revenue to creator before any outside checkout/payment costs; referrals and sales are separate economics.
- Webflow: 95% commission on paid marketplace templates, with application and content vetting.
- Shopify App Store: qualified developers pay no platform revenue share on their first $1,000,000 of lifetime eligible gross app revenue, then 15%; an extra 2.9% processing and possible taxes/fees apply. Marketplace listing has registration/review requirements; 0% commission does **not** mean no expenses.
- itch.io: configurable platform revenue share (often 10% default) + payment processor fees.
- Lemon Squeezy: base 5% + $0.50; additional fees can apply. A merchant-of-record sales checkout is not a source of free customers.
- Gumroad: direct-link base 10% + $0.50; marketplace Discover has a substantially higher percentage; processor rules differ by sale method.
- Fiverr: sellers keep 80% of completed order value. Product quality and manual revisions are important.
- Etsy: 6.5% transaction fee + $0.20 listing + processing + potential advertising/other costs; seller-prompted AI must be disclosed, and production partners disclosed. Etsy API supports listing drafts and digital file uploads; selling apps to *other* Etsy shops needs a different access track from managing one's own shop.
- B2B demand: a 2026 Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses Voices survey of 1,256 participating owners reported 76% using AI but only 14% fully integrated it; 73% sought help/training. This is not a representative population estimate nor proof anyone will buy STAR's services. It supports direct customer discovery.

## Launch order

**First 14 days:** Validate the Roblox UI audit/plugin and a fixed-scope Roblox UI/bug-fix service. Use organic, permission-respecting research and outreach. Build one product, not 40.  
**Days 15–45:** Publish a verified original asset or plugin after the owner's commercial account is set up, prepare the service portfolio, and try first legitimate client deliveries.  
**Days 46–90:** Add one independent storefront (Lemon Squeezy or another supported checkout) and a complementary website template. Only trial subscription service when recurring demand exists.  
**Quarter 2:** Port successful systems to Unity/Godot or other marketplaces if support requirements and demand justify the cost. No expansion until the first two lines have evidence of buyer traction.

## Scoring model

Score each candidate on five 0–5 scales: **observable buyer pain (30%)**, **ability to deliver verified quality (25%)**, **customer access/distribution (20%)**, **contribution margin (15%)**, and **maintenance/compliance feasibility (10%)**. These are decision-support inputs, not statistical forecasts. Add a `verified_evidence_count` and separate `unverified_assumptions` list. Require evidence from real prospective customers before production.

A Stop/Scale decision occurs after sufficient exposure, not merely elapsed days: track qualified visits, inquiries, successful checkouts, fulfilled orders, refunds, defect rate, contribution margin and hours spent. Low traffic means *insufficient evidence*, not automatic proof a product is bad.

## STAR Business OS architecture

The public STAR `star/next` source has a React/TypeScript desktop frontend, Python core, local/remote model routing, Roblox Studio MCP, policy enforcement, knowledge, lesson gates and local analytics. The current `star/orchestration.py` explicitly describes itself as a **deterministic, nonexecuting dependency scheduler**. It validates DAGs and file-scope ownership with `TaskPlan`; it **does not spawn subagents**. Its default is 2 parallel slots and it accepts 1–8. `star/policy.py` classifies allowed Studio tool capabilities and disallows unknown/unapproved tools. New commercial functions should preserve these security boundaries.

### Proposed package layout (not present yet)

```text
star/business/
  contracts.py             # idea, business, job, product, asset, order, refund, invoice schemas
  validation.py            # market evidence + buyer discovery score, QA and cost gates
  orchestrator.py          # durable task state, leases, idempotent retries
  workers.py               # bound and audited workers using existing model/provider loop
  finance.py               # append-only validated financial and cost events
  pricing.py               # fee schedules, margins, thresholds, price experiments
  approval.py              # commercial-action approval policies and expiring grants
  storefronts/
    etsy.py                # OAuth scoped shop, draft listings, approved activation
    printify.py            # product drafts and fulfillment policy
    roblox.py              # draft artifact and manual publishing checklist
    lemonsqueezy.py        # own-product checkout events, webhook verification
    shopify.py             # only after app/merchant authorization and review
  QA/
    license_check.py       # ownable, sublicensable, attributable inputs
    deliverable_check.py   # deterministic source, metadata, visual, docs and test gates
    platform_policy.py     # platform and category restrictions
  reports.py               # performance, contribution margin, stopped experiments
frontend/
  BusinessDashboard         # visual, evidence-grounded workspace
  IdeaBoard                 # scored proposals + market evidence source cards
  WorkOrders                # verified agent queue and customer task proof
  QualityReview             # screenshots/tests, rejection and corrections
  Approvals                 # owner confirm, expiry and reason
  FinancialLedger           # reconciled revenue, cash, profit and expense ledgers
```

Keep source of truth under `%APPDATA%/STAR` or a protected dedicated local data directory; **not** in the installer folder or a public git repository. Use local SQLite with WAL, schema migrations and encrypted OS-secret storage; for remote multi-machine execution add a protected service with clear tenancy rules. A queued job must be idempotent, survive reboot, have a retry cap and never double charge or double publish. Record `run_id`, `business_id`, `tool`, `actor`, `authorization`, `idempotency_key`, `evidence_hash`, `cost`, `created_at` and `result` for every step.

### Mandatory workflow and commercial gates

```text
IDEA_RESEARCHED
  -> BUYER_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED
  -> OWNER_APPROVES_BUDGET
  -> MVP_BUILDING
  -> STATIC_QA -> RUNTIME_QA -> VISUAL_QA -> RIGHTS_QA
  -> OWNER_REVIEWS_LISTING
  -> MARKETPLACE_DRAFT
  -> OWNER_APPROVES_PUBLICATION
  -> LISTED
  -> AUTHENTIC_ORDER_RECEIVED
  -> FULFILLMENT_QA
  -> SHIPPED / DELIVERED
  -> SUPPORT_AND_MEASUREMENT
  -> IMPROVE / PAUSE / RETIRE
```

Commerce agents cannot approve their own budget or edit safety graders. A generative LLM does not receive platform secrets, payment credentials, an unrestricted shell in merchant accounts or an unbounded send-mail tool. Untrusted product listing content must not be allowed to change tool permissions. Storefront draft writes should be separate from irreversible publishing. Every external message must have consent/anti-spam and platform-policy checks. Payouts and refunds must not be initiated autonomously.

### Tool strategy

- **Python first, local models first:** Reuse `star/agent.py`, providers, `star/policy.py`, memory and tests; route simple classification to a cheaper local model and complex design to best available authorized model. Benchmark output quality rather than assuming free local models produce premium assets.
- **Workflow reliability:** In the MVP, use durable SQLite-backed task leases/checkpoints. Evaluate LangGraph for graph checkpoints and approval pause/resume, Temporal for production-grade long-running workflows only if the system needs that complexity. Avoid running multiple overlapping workflow engines on day 1.
- **External connectors:** Etsy API Seller App for the owner's own shop; Printify through officially supported endpoints/integration; Stripe-compatible approved checkout for digital goods; Roblox Creator Store through supported publishing flows, otherwise generate verified drafts for manual publishing. Authenticate OAuth scopes; audit per-endpoint access. Verify webhook signatures.
- **n8n:** Suitable for internal client workflows and rapid prototypes, but its Sustainable Use License is not an OSI open-source license and outside customers configuring your hosted n8n workflows may require a commercial license.
- **Security:** Strict scope, independently reviewed policy changes, no self-edited QA fixtures, sandbox assets, protected payment events, source provenance, data deletion, secrets in OS vault and rate limits.

## Financial truth / unit economics

For physical product/order `i`, estimate:

`contribution_i = collected_revenue_i - refunds_i - marketplace_fees_i - processor_fees_i - production_i - shipping_i - ad_attribution_i - AI_generation_i - delivery_labor_i`

Then: `operating_profit = sum(contribution_i) - fixed_hosting - software_subscriptions - accounting - other overhead` (before business-specific tax provisions). Separate cash flow, gross revenue, payout pending, refunds and profit. Tax collection remitted by a marketplace is not seller earned revenue. Human support hours have a cost even if the owner performs them.

### Scenarios (illustrative arithmetic, not predictions)

- **3 service projects at $199**: $597 gross revenue. If sold through Fiverr, seller share is about `$597 * 80% = $477.60` before delivery costs and taxes; direct sales have different checkout fees and client acquisition costs.
- **20 paid model/plugin sales at $24.99**: $499.80 gross; on Roblox, processing/tax are deducted and proceeds may sit in escrow for 30 days. No assumed number of buyers.
- **5 template sales at $39**: $195 gross; Framer creator terms say 100% of Marketplace earnings but external checkout fees still exist. No assumed monthly reliability.
- **5 B2B monthly clients paying $49**: $245 MRR gross, but net depends heavily on retention, support, infrastructure and acquisition.

All scenarios can be **$0** in practice if no one buys. Never confuse this with a likely earnings projection.

## First 14-day execution plan

**Days 1–2:** Select 2 target segments (Roblox developers; small businesses needing a narrow workflow). Collect 20 real pain statements with source URLs and make 5 targeted, permission-respecting conversations. Avoid mass DMs, unsolicited bulk email and scraping protected personal information.

**Days 3–4:** Interview or message 5–10 potential buyers with permission. Ask what problem consumes hours, how they solve it now, whether they'd use a test pilot, what concrete evidence would earn trust, and what budget they have.

**Days 5–8:** Build one paid-quality MVP: recommended choice is a Roblox UI accessibility/QA auditor. Target 6–10 well-defined diagnostics, a non-destructive plugin UI, documentation and a demonstration test place. Use real Roblox Studio, run official Luau/static checks and keep an issue/QA ledger. Show what it misses as well as what it finds.

**Days 9–10:** Create demos and a limited free edition or free verified report. Run 3 external pilot reviews. Refine false positives and performance before requesting publication approvals.

**Days 11–12:** Prepare Creator Store seller onboarding and listing draft (if eligible) OR a fixed-price commissioned UI/bug-fix service page. Collect screenshots, a support email, changelog and clear refund/support scope.

**Days 13–14:** Owner approves storefront, price, first publication and any prospective client messages. Launch one pilot; watch *qualified* visits, messages, demos, actual paid orders and support issues. Review the next move, not a fabricated earnings target.

## Hard boundaries and risks

- No guaranteed, passive or unattended income claim. Real audience, support and product-market fit are necessary.
- Do not clone competitors' visual designs, copyrighted listings, paid assets or trademarked branding; use competitive information to understand jobs-to-be-done, then produce original work.
- Unreviewed AI images can contain defects and licensing questions; check image/tool/model output rights and human creative contributions. Source of all asset pack content must be documented.
- Etsy expects original seller designs, AI disclosure where applicable and production partner disclosure; Shopify/Unity/Roblox/Fab have category, review and seller eligibility requirements.
- YouTube penalizes generic mass-produced/repetitive content for monetization; don't base the business on indiscriminate AI slideshows.
- Affiliate marketing requires clear, conspicuous commission disclosures, per FTC guidance. Never fake testimonials or reviews.
- Treat third-party content and agent output as untrusted; commercial agents must not decide to lift their own spending or tool permissions.
- If a connected client data source contains private information, use its permitted access scope, minimize retention and obtain consent to process the data. Avoid regulated advice/decisions outside expertise.
- STAR's public source does not presently contain a top-level explicit software license in the reviewed README. Confirm the license and publication strategy before planning commercial reuse or selling STAR-derived code as a separate product.
