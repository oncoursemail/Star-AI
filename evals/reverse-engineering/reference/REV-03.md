Short version: recreate the mechanics, don't take the assets or the leaked code. (This is general information, not legal advice; for anything commercial talk to a lawyer.)

- Owning the CD gives you a licence to play your copy, not to redistribute its contents. Don't upload the original sprites, music or sounds to Roblox: that is distributing copyrighted art and audio, and the rights holder can have the experience taken down.
- Don't use or even read the leaked source. Leaked code is someone else's copyrighted, unlawfully released work; serious reconstruction projects such as the N64 decomp teams refuse to touch leaks to keep "clean hands", and Wine bars contributors who have seen Microsoft source from working on the affected components.
- How OpenRCT2 and OpenTTD handle it: they ship only their own code and require players to bring their own copy of the original game data files; the engine loads the user's original data at runtime and the project never distributes it. OpenTTD also gained free replacement graphics, sound and music sets so it can run without the original at all. Roblox has no "bring your own data" option (everything you use must be uploaded), so the Roblox equivalent is the replacement-asset route.
- What you can do: study how the game plays and write down the rules, the numbers you observe and the feel (a spec), then build those mechanics yourself in Luau. Game mechanics and ideas are generally not protected the way code, art, music and names are. Make your own art and music (or properly licensed assets), and use a different name and logo to avoid trademark problems.
